**Polish Christmas Eve food changes from region to region. The frame is the same everywhere (a meatless supper that begins with the first star and the sharing of opłatek), but the dishes depend on local rivers, forests and harvests: gingerbread moczka and poppy seed makówki in Silesia, barley with swede and honey (kołatanka) in Podhale, dried-fruit soup with noodles in Kashubia, potato rwaki in Masovia, wild-mushroom barszcz in Podlasie, and poppy seed noodles in Greater Poland.** Treat the dishes below as examples, not rules: tables differ from village to village and from family to family.

New to the tradition? Start with [What Is Wigilia?](/blog/cooking/01-what-is-wigilia/), then see [the 12 dishes](/blog/cooking/02-12-dishes-polish-christmas-eve/) and our [week-by-week planning guide](/blog/cooking/04-plan-12-dish-wigilia-feast/). This article is the regional map.

## Silesia (Śląsk)

Silesia has some of the best-known sweet Christmas Eve dishes. **Moczka**, also called *bryja*, is made from a special gingerbread soaked with almonds, raisins, dried plums, apricots, figs and other fruit in dark beer. Some modern versions skip the beer and use fruit and compotes instead. **Makówki** are layers of sweet bread and poppy seeds boiled in milk with butter, with dried fruit and nuts, served cold; in Silesia they are served almost exclusively on Christmas Eve, and outside the region they are known as *makiełki*, for example in Poznań and Łódź. Silesian tables also feature **siemieniotka**, a linseed soup.

## Podhale and Małopolska

In the Tatra highlands of **Podhale**, Culture.pl describes *kołatanka*: cooked barley mixed with swede and honey. Elsewhere in **Małopolska** (Lesser Poland), Polish at Heart lists mushroom soup (*zupa grzybowa*) with square homemade pasta (*łazanki*), potato flatbreads called *moskole* served with mushrooms, and trout, especially in the mountains. Culture.pl adds *fizioły*, big white beans cooked with smoked prunes, rosemary and honey, from Małopolska and the Pieniny.

## Kashubia and Pomerania

In **Kashubia**, supper traditionally opens with a dried-fruit soup with noodles, eaten instead of barszcz, according to Pomorskie Travel. Up to ten meatless dishes are served from peas, beans, poppy seed, dried mushrooms and fruit, cabbage and potatoes; herring and eel are common, and *kuch*, a yeast cake, is a traditional treat. Culture.pl also describes *brzad*, a soup of boiled dried fruit served with small dumplings, from Kashubia and Kociewie. On the coast of **Pomerania**, Polish at Heart lists herring with apples and a fish soup with herring, and Culture.pl mentions *sieja* (whitefish) fried and served with a mushroom sauce.

## Masovia (Mazowsze)

Polish at Heart lists white barszcz (a soup with cabbage and mushrooms), *rwaki* (potato dumplings with cabbage and mushrooms), pike-perch (*sandacz*), cranberry *kisiel* (a homemade jelly) and herring in cream. Culture.pl describes *rwaki* as dumplings made of boiled potatoes, flour and eggs.

## Podlasie

In the east, Polish at Heart describes barszcz made on wild mushrooms, local *wareniki* (pierogi shaped by Belarusian, Ukrainian, Jewish and Tatar influences) and a forest-raspberry compote sweetened with honey. The old eastern borderlands (Kresy) are also the home of **kutia**: boiled wheat with poppy seed, honey and nuts, traditionally eaten first, right after the opłatek, according to Sophie Hodorowicz Knab. A regional saying goes, "There is no Wigilia without kucja."

## Greater Poland (Wielkopolska)

Region Wielkopolska lists fruit soup with dried apples and prunes served with small noodles, poppy-seed noodles, herring in cream and in oil, cabbage with white beans, baked carp, mushrooms roasted in butter, stewed prunes, gingerbread and poppy-seed cake. Borscht with uszka, mushroom soup with łazanki, kutia and pierogi are newer additions that came through Western influence, media, migration and marriage. A masked visitor called Gwiazdor, in a sheepskin coat worn inside out, traditionally brings the presents after supper.

## Build a regional menu at home

- **Pick a region** that matches your family roots or curiosity, and cook two or three of its dishes.
- **Fill the rest of the twelve** with the classics from our [12-dish guide](/blog/cooking/02-12-dishes-polish-christmas-eve/).
- **Substitute freely** where an ingredient is hard to find. Trout or cod are common stand-ins for carp.

## Key takeaways

- Wigilia shares one frame across Poland, but **the dishes are regional**.
- **Silesia:** moczka and makówki. **Podhale:** kołatanka. **Kashubia:** dried-fruit soup. **Masovia:** rwaki. **Podlasie:** wild-mushroom barszcz. **Greater Poland:** poppy-seed noodles.
- Kutia comes from the **eastern borderlands** and is now served widely.
- Use the regions as inspiration; **traditions vary by village and family**.
