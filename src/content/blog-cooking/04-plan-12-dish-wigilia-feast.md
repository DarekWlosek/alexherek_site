**Plan a 12-dish Wigilia in four stages: settle the menu about four weeks out, start the long-lead items (the beet starter for barszcz and, if you like, piernik) in early December or earlier, shape and freeze the dumplings the week before, and cook the rest on December 22 and 23, so Christmas Eve itself is mostly reheating and boiling.** In 2026, Christmas Eve falls on Thursday, December 24, and the dinner traditionally begins when the first star appears.

This article is part of our Wigilia series. If the tradition is new to you, start with [What Is Wigilia?](/blog/cooking/01-what-is-wigilia/); for the full menu see [What Are the 12 Dishes of Polish Christmas Eve?](/blog/cooking/02-12-dishes-polish-christmas-eve/), and for step-by-step recipes see [Traditional Polish Christmas Recipes](/blog/cooking/03-traditional-polish-christmas-recipes/).

## Quick answer

- **Start early, cook late.** Only a few items need weeks (piernik, zakwas, homemade sauerkraut). Most dishes are cooked in the last two or three days.
- **Freeze what you can.** Uszka (small mushroom dumplings) and pierogi freeze raw; barszcz freezes once cooled.
- **Twelve is a tradition, not a test.** Custom says to taste every dish, so portions stay small, and there is no official list of twelve.
- **Keep the day itself light.** On December 24 you only reheat, boil, fry and set the table before the first star.

## The plan, stage by stage

### Four weeks before: decide

Write down your twelve dishes, then cross off anything that does not earn its place. The table in [our menu guide](/blog/cooking/02-12-dishes-polish-christmas-eve/) is a good starting point. Count guests, note vegetarian or vegan eaters, and decide which dishes you will buy rather than make. A realistic menu has a few showpieces and several simple dishes.

### Three weeks before: the slow starters

If you want piernik staropolski, Polonist's menu guide suggests starting around November 6 and no later than December 10, because the cake matures. The beet starter for barszcz, called zakwas, is started between December 3 and 10 in the same guide, and Polonist's barszcz recipe notes that it can be made weeks in advance. If you ferment your own sauerkraut for the pierogi filling, allow at least 10 days (Polish Your Kitchen).

### Two weeks before: shop and make room

Buy the dry goods that keep: dried mushrooms (porcini are the closest substitute outside Poland), poppy seed, wheat berries, honey and dried fruit for kompot (a dried-fruit drink). Check the freezer, because next week you will fill it.

### One week before: shape and freeze

Polonist places the making of uszka in the week leading up to December 24. Shape uszka and pierogi, freeze them raw on a tray, then bag them. If you plan to serve carp, many Polish families buy it only a few days before Christmas Eve, and some buy it live (TalkPolish).

### December 22 and 23: the big cook

This is our suggested split, not a rule. On December 22, bake makowiec (it slices better after a day), soak the wheat berries and prepare herring. On December 23, make kutia (a wheat-berry pudding) and barszcz, braise the sauerkraut and cook the fish dishes you plan to serve cold.

## Christmas Eve: the run of show

1. **Morning.** Take the frozen uszka and pierogi out only when you are ready to boil them; set out the serving dishes.
2. **Afternoon.** Fry the carp and warm the sauerkraut. Reheat barszcz slowly over low heat; Polonist advises against boiling or microwaving it, and notes it is best eaten within three hours of serving.
3. **Before dark.** Set the table. Lay one extra place for an unexpected guest, a custom that stands for hospitality (Wikipedia, In Your Pocket).
4. **At the first star.** Begin by sharing opłatek, then serve the soup, the fish and savory dishes, and the sweets. Many families go to pasterka, the midnight Mass, afterward.

## Planning tips outside Poland

- **Carp is hard to find.** Trout or cod work, and you can buy fresh fish on December 22 or 23.
- **Order specialty items early.** Dried porcini, poppy seed and good sauerkraut sell out near Christmas in some places.
- **Use shortcuts without guilt.** Store-bought poppy seed filling for makowiec saves an afternoon.

## Key takeaways

- Count back from **Thursday, December 24, 2026**, and start the slow items first: **piernik, zakwas and homemade sauerkraut**.
- **Shape and freeze** uszka and pierogi in the week before; cook most other dishes on **December 22 and 23**.
- Barszcz can be cooked **one day ahead**, kept in the refrigerator or frozen, and reheated **gently**.
- Twelve dishes is a **tradition, not a rule**; buy what you do not have time to make.
