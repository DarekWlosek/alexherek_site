**Quick answer:** Wigilia, the supper on December 24, and Christmas Day dinner on December 25, are two different meals with two different moods. Wigilia is meatless and built around twelve symbolic dishes — carp, barszcz with mushroom-filled dumplings (*uszka*), and pierogi among them — eaten the evening before Christmas as the end of the Advent fast. Christmas Day is a relaxed family lunch that can include meat dishes such as bigos or roast duck, along with alcohol, which is traditionally set aside the night before. The two meals share a holiday spirit, but not a menu.

## Wigilia: the meatless supper of December 24

Wigilia is the main event of the Polish Christmas season, held on the evening of December 24. According to In Your Pocket's guide to Christmas in Warsaw, Wigilia is "meant to be meatless," and carp — the dish most associated with the evening — is not counted as meat in this tradition. The supper traditionally includes twelve dishes, with fish, mushrooms, beets, cabbage, dried fruit and grains forming the backbone of the table rather than any roasted or fried meat. For the full rundown of what actually appears on the table, see our guide to [the fish of Wigilia](/blog/cooking/07-polish-christmas-eve-fish-dishes/) and our [regional variations guide](/blog/cooking/05-polish-christmas-eve-food-by-region/).

The evening carries a quieter, more formal character than the day that follows it. Families wait for the first star to appear before sitting down to eat, share the Christmas wafer (*opłatek*), and set an extra place at the table for an unexpected guest. Alcohol is traditionally avoided during the meal itself — a custom that, as In Your Pocket notes, is "commonly overlooked" in practice, but one that still marks Wigilia as a more restrained evening than what comes next.

## Christmas Day: a relaxed, meat-filled family lunch

December 25 is a different occasion entirely. Polish Your Kitchen describes Christmas Day as "more relaxed" than Wigilia, a day typically spent with close family over a hearty lunch. Unlike the evening before, meat returns to the table. Polish Your Kitchen specifically mentions bigos (a slow-cooked hunter's stew of cabbage and meat), roasted duck and czernina (a traditional duck blood soup) as dishes that can appear once the fasting period of Wigilia has passed. In Your Pocket puts it simply: the days that follow Wigilia "can (and are) be filled with succulent meat dishes," and alcohol — set aside the night before — becomes part of the celebration again.

Where Wigilia is built around a fixed, symbolic set of twelve dishes, Christmas Day has no such structure. It is a lunch of family favorites rather than a ritual meal, and what is served varies far more from household to household.

## Wigilia vs. Christmas Day at a glance

| | Wigilia (Dec 24) | Christmas Day (Dec 25) |
|---|---|---|
| **Character** | Formal, reflective evening supper | Relaxed family lunch |
| **Meat** | Avoided — pescatarian and plant-based | Included — meat dishes are typical |
| **Typical dishes** | Carp, barszcz z uszkami, pierogi, herring | Bigos, roast duck, czernina |
| **Number of dishes** | Traditionally twelve, symbolic | No fixed number |
| **Alcohol** | Traditionally avoided | Included |
| **Timing** | Evening of Dec 24, after the first star | Daytime on Dec 25 |

## Dishes that can appear at both meals

A few dishes cross over between the two days. Pierogi, for example, can appear at Wigilia in a meatless form (filled with sauerkraut and mushrooms, or potato and cheese) and again on Christmas Day in a meat-filled version. The overlap is really about shared ingredients and family recipes carrying through the holiday, not about the two menus being interchangeable — Wigilia keeps its fish-and-fasting character throughout, while Christmas Day is free to include whatever the family typically serves for a special lunch.

## Key takeaways

- **Wigilia (Dec 24)** is meatless, formal, and built around twelve symbolic dishes.
- **Christmas Day (Dec 25)** is a relaxed lunch that typically includes meat and alcohol.
- **Bigos, roast duck and czernina** are Christmas Day dishes, not Wigilia dishes.
- **Pierogi** can appear at both meals, in meatless or meat-filled form.
- The two meals share a holiday but not a menu — plan them separately.
