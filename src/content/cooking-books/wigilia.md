## Overview

When the first star appears, the Polish Christmas table comes alive. For
generations, Wigilia — the Polish Christmas Eve vigil — has been more than
a dinner. It's a choreography of small, deliberate acts: hay spread beneath
a white tablecloth, one extra place set for the unexpected guest, a thin
wafer broken and shared, a sky watched patiently for the first star.

This illustrated cookbook tells the story of Wigilia through three historic
tables — the humble peasant table, the elegant manor table of the szlachta,
and the lavish royal table of the Polish court — before traveling the map
of Poland itself across six regions.

## One Night, Three Tables

Travel from a candlelit peasant cottage, where twelve dishes were assembled
from cabbage, mushrooms, and groats, to the manor houses of the szlachta,
where the same fast was observed with silver and porcelain, to the royal
kitchens of Wawel Castle, where saffron, almonds, and Madeira transformed a
meatless vigil into a spectacle of majesty. The fast never changed. The
table always did.

## Six Regions of Poland

Silesia's gingerbread dessert soups. Podhale's highland mushroom broths.
Kashubia's hemp seed soup and potato pancakes. Mazovia's herring and
buckwheat. Podlasie's forest mushrooms and fermented bread drink. Greater
Poland's sourdough and horseradish. Each region brought its own rivers,
forests, and harvests to the same twelve-dish table.

## 150 Recipes, Kitchen-Ready

Every recipe is laid out one per page with clear ingredients, numbered
instructions, variations and substitutes, and a History/Fun Fact note
explaining the folklore behind the dish. Imperial and metric measurements
are given side by side. Dietary labels — vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free —
mark each recipe where applicable.

## Built for the Global Kitchen

Can't find carp? Use trout or cod. No bryndza? Feta and farmer's cheese
work beautifully. Dried wild mushrooms? Dried porcini are an excellent
substitute. Every recipe includes ingredient substitutions designed for
the Polish diaspora and curious cooks worldwide.

- Polish-Americans and the global diaspora recreating a family table from memory

- History and culture enthusiasts who want the stories behind the food
- Vegetarian, vegan, and gluten-free cooks who want to take part in Wigilia
